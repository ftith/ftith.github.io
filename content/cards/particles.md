 | Kana | Romaji | Explanation | Example |
 | --- | --- | --- | --- |
 | は | wa | Marks the topic of the sentence: what the sentence is about. Written は but pronounced *wa* as a particle. | 私<span style="color:red">は</span>学生です。<br>わたし<span style="color:red">は</span>がくせいです。<br>I am a student. |
 | が | ga | Marks the grammatical subject, often identifying or emphasizing who or what does something. | 猫<span style="color:red">が</span>います。<br>ねこ<span style="color:red">が</span>います。<br>There is a cat. |
 | を | o (wo) | Marks the direct object of an action. Written を and traditionally romanized *wo*, but usually pronounced *o*. | 水<span style="color:red">を</span>飲みます。<br>みず<span style="color:red">を</span>のみます。<br>I drink water. |
 | に | ni | Marks a destination, a specific time, a recipient, or the location where something exists. | 7時<span style="color:red">に</span>起きます。<br>しちじ<span style="color:red">に</span>おきます。<br>I get up at seven. |
 | で | de | Marks where an action takes place or the means or method used to do it. | 図書館<span style="color:red">で</span>勉強します。<br>としょかん<span style="color:red">で</span>べんきょうします。<br>I study at the library. |
 | へ | e | Marks the direction of movement, meaning “toward.” Written へ but pronounced *e* as a particle. | 学校<span style="color:red">へ</span>行きます。<br>がっこう<span style="color:red">へ</span>いきます。<br>I go toward school. |
 | の | no | Connects nouns, often showing possession or describing one noun with another. | 私<span style="color:red">の</span>本です。<br>わたし<span style="color:red">の</span>ほんです。<br>It is my book. |
 | と | to | Means “and” when listing things, “with” when describing company, or marks a quotation. | 友達<span style="color:red">と</span>行きます。<br>ともだち<span style="color:red">と</span>いきます。<br>I go with a friend. |
 | も | mo | Means “also” or “too”; it can replace は or が to include another person or thing. | 私<span style="color:red">も</span>行きます。<br>わたし<span style="color:red">も</span>いきます。<br>I am going too. |
 | から | kara | Marks a starting point in time or place; can also mean “because” in some sentence patterns. | 9時<span style="color:red">から</span>働きます。<br>くじ<span style="color:red">から</span>はたらきます。<br>I work from nine o’clock. |
 | まで | made | Marks an endpoint in time or place, meaning “until” or “as far as.” | 5時<span style="color:red">まで</span>働きます。<br>ごじ<span style="color:red">まで</span>はたらきます。<br>I work until five o’clock. |
 | や | ya | Connects examples in a non-exhaustive list, meaning “and things like.” | りんご<span style="color:red">や</span>バナナ<span style="color:red">を</span>買いました。<br>りんご<span style="color:red">や</span>ばなな<span style="color:red">を</span>かいました。<br>I bought apples, bananas, and things like that. |
 | か | ka | Marks a question at the end of a sentence or means “or” between choices. | 学生です<span style="color:red">か</span>。<br>がくせいです<span style="color:red">か</span>。<br>Are you a student? |
 | ね | ne | Seeks agreement or shares a feeling, similar to “right?” or “isn’t it?” | いい天気です<span style="color:red">ね</span>。<br>いいてんきです<span style="color:red">ね</span>。<br>The weather is nice, isn't it? |
 | よ | yo | Adds emphasis or tells the listener something they may not know. | これ<span style="color:red">は</span>おいしいです<span style="color:red">よ</span>。<br>これ<span style="color:red">は</span>おいしいです<span style="color:red">よ</span>。<br>This is delicious, you know. |
 | だけ | dake | Means “only” or “just,” limiting what it refers to. | 水<span style="color:red">だけ</span>飲みます。<br>みず<span style="color:red">だけ</span>のみます。<br>I drink only water. |
 | しか | shika | Means “only” or “nothing but” and is used with a negative verb. | 100円<span style="color:red">しか</span>ありません。<br>ひゃくえん<span style="color:red">しか</span>ありません。<br>I have only 100 yen. |
 | より | yori | Marks a comparison point, meaning “than”; it can also mean “from” in formal usage. | 犬<span style="color:red">より</span>猫<span style="color:red">が</span>好きです。<br>いぬ<span style="color:red">より</span>ねこ<span style="color:red">が</span>すきです。<br>I like cats more than dogs. |
